APF keeps little state on a host. Most of what it needs, the signed bundle, trust roots, and keys, comes from your deployment and publish pipelines and can be reinstalled. The anti-rollback counter is the one piece of state that only the host has, and it needs deliberate handling.

## State on a Host

| State | Location on a systemd host | Source of truth | Back up? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service settings | `/etc/apf/*.env` | Your deployment pipeline. | Rebuild from the pipeline. |
| Signed bundle | `/etc/apf/bundle/` | Your artifact store. | Rebuild from the artifact store. |
| Trust roots | `/etc/apf/trust-roots/` | Your signing authority. | Rebuild from the pipeline. |
| Document signing key, TLS keys, tokens | `/etc/apf/document-signing-key`, `/etc/apf/tls/`, `/etc/apf/http-bearer-token` | Your secret manager. | Reissue or restore from the secret manager. Never copy into ordinary backups. |
| Extension trust profiles | `/etc/apf/openshell-*-auth.toml` | The OpenShell gateway's configuration. | Rebuild from the pipeline. |
| Anti-rollback counter | `/var/lib/apf/policy-service/counter` and `counter.digest` | The host. | Yes. See below. |
| Audit records | The audit directories | Your log pipeline, once shipped. | Ship off the host. |

The policy gateway keeps no policy state of its own. It rebuilds its evaluator from the policy service at startup.

## The Anti-Rollback Counter

The counter records the highest bundle revision the policy service accepted. Losing it, or restoring an older copy, lets the host accept an older signed bundle again.

- **Back it up after each activation**, or at least as often as you publish bundles.
- **Treat counter loss as a security event.** A missing counter reads as `0`, as on a fresh install. Before you restart the service, make sure the only bundle it can load is the current one.
- **Do not restore an older counter** to work around a rejection. Publish a bundle with a higher revision instead.
- **A corrupt counter stops the service.** It refuses to start until an operator restores a good copy or removes the files deliberately.

## Recovery Scenarios

| Situation | What happens | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| The policy service stops while the gateway runs. | The gateway keeps deciding requests with its current evaluator and logs failed polls. New sandbox admissions fail closed because the interceptor cannot fetch the projection. | Restart the policy service. |
| The gateway restarts while the policy service is down. | The gateway cannot fetch the policy set and does not start. With `fail_closed` registrations, OpenShell refuses governed operations. | Start the policy service first, then the gateway. |
| A bad bundle is delivered by refresh or poll. | The policy service keeps the current bundle. A verification failure is recorded. A compilation or fetch failure is only logged. | Fix the bundle and publish a higher revision. Refer to [Update Policies](../manage-policy-bundles/update-policies.mdx#recover-from-a-rejected-update). |
| A bad bundle is installed in place and the policy service restarts. | The policy service rejects the bundle and does not start. | Restore the previous bundle files, or install a fixed bundle with a higher revision. |
| The active bundle expires and the policy service restarts. | The policy service rejects the bundle and does not start. | Publish a re-signed bundle with a later `expires_at` and a higher revision. |
| A host is rebuilt. | All local state is gone. | Reinstall from your pipelines, restore the counter from backup, then start the services. |
| A signing key is compromised. | Bundles signed with it are accepted until its trust root is removed. | Sign a replacement bundle with a new key, put it where each policy service loads it at startup, install the new trust root, remove the compromised one, and restart. Refer to [Signing Keys and Trust Roots](../security/signing-keys-and-trust-roots.mdx). |

## Backup Tooling

The repository does not include backup or restore tooling. Use your platform's tools, and keep backups of the counter under the same access controls as the counter itself.
