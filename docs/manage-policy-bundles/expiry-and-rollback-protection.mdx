A signature proves a bundle is authentic. It does not prove the bundle is current. APF adds two freshness controls: a monotonic revision counter that refuses older bundles, and a validity window that bounds when a bundle may be accepted.

## The Revision Counter

Every bundle carries a `policy_revision`. The policy service keeps the highest revision it has accepted, called the high-water mark, and the digest of the payload it accepted at that revision.

| Candidate bundle | Result |
|---|---|
| Revision higher than the high-water mark | Accepted. The new revision and payload digest are recorded. |
| Same revision, same payload | Accepted. This lets a restart reload the bundle the service was already serving, even if the artifact was re-signed. |
| Same revision, different payload | Rejected with `counter_content_mismatch`. |
| Lower revision | Rejected with `counter_regression`. |

The counter binds to the payload digest, not the artifact digest, so re-signing identical content does not look like a change. A revision is recorded only after the bundle verifies and compiles.

<Note>
On a fresh counter store the high-water mark is `0`, and revision `0` is rejected as a regression. Start every bundle at revision `1` or higher.
</Note>

### The Counter Store

The default counter store is a pair of files at `--counter-store`, `/var/lib/apf/counter` by default:

- `<path>` holds the high-water mark as a single decimal line.
- `<path>.digest` holds `<revision> <sha256>` for the accepted payload.

Writes go to a temporary file that is then renamed, so a crash leaves either the old or the new value. If the two files disagree after a crash, or the digest file is missing, the digest is treated as unknown rather than causing a spurious rejection. The next bundle accepted at the current revision is then accepted whatever its content, normally the reload at startup, and its digest is recorded again.

| Store state | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Missing | The high-water mark is `0`, as on a fresh install. |
| Present and valid | Loaded at startup. |
| Corrupt | The policy service refuses to start. An operator must intervene. |

The file store suits one policy service per host. It is not shared between policy services.

### Counter Scope

- Each policy service has its own counter. Two hosts can accept the same revision independently.
- All bundles a policy service loads share one counter, whatever their `id`. Use one revision sequence per policy service.
- `apf-compile compile` uses a temporary counter, so it cannot detect a revision that a running service would reject.
- The policy gateway keeps no counter. It trusts the policy service.

### Resetting the Counter

Removing the counter files resets the high-water mark to `0` and lets any revision load again. That reopens the window for rollback, so treat it as a privileged recovery action, for example when you rebuild a host for a new environment. The counter directory should be writable only by the `apf` account.

## The Validity Window

`not_before` and `expires_at` bound when a bundle may be accepted. Both are RFC 3339 times.

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Ingested before `not_before` | Rejected with `bundle_not_yet_valid`. |
| Ingested after `expires_at` | Rejected with `bundle_expired`. |
| Expires while active | Keeps being served until it is replaced. The next restart or reload rejects it. |

Because expiry is checked at ingest, plan renewals ahead of time: publish a re-signed bundle with a higher revision and a later `expires_at` well before the current one expires. If a policy service restarts after its only bundle has expired, it does not start, and with `fail_closed` registrations OpenShell refuses the operations APF governs.

## Document Validity

Separately from the bundle's validity window, the policy service can limit how long a served document may be cached. With `--document-ttl-secs` set, each `GetDocument` response carries `valid_until_ms`, and the provenance token carries a matching `exp`. The interceptor rejects an expired provenance token. APF's sandbox policy refresher arms a single refresh 30 seconds before the deadline it learns from a policy set change. It does not arm one at gateway startup, and it fires once, so do not rely on it to keep long-running sandboxes refreshed. The default, `0`, sets no document expiry.

## What These Controls Stop

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| Replaying an older, superseded bundle | The counter rejects a lower revision. |
| Swapping different content in at an accepted revision | The counter rejects a different payload at the same revision. |
| Using a bundle before it was meant to take effect | `not_before`. |
| Accepting a bundle long after it was meant to retire | `expires_at`, checked at ingest. |
| Keeping a superseded bundle in service | Publish a higher revision; the counter refuses the lower one on the next load. A running policy service keeps serving its current bundle until it loads a replacement or restarts, so deliver the new revision to every host and reload. |
